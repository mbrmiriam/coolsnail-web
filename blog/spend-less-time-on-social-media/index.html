How to spend less time on social media without quitting

By Miriam, who makes Cool Snail · Updated 17 October 2026

Short answer

Willpower alone rarely works, because feeds are built to keep you there. What helps is making scrolling less automatic: see where your time really goes, turn off notifications, move the apps out of reach, keep the phone out of the bedroom, and remove the endless parts (Reels, Shorts, infinite feeds) instead of your whole account. You keep your messages and friends; you lose the hole. On Android, a free app like Cool Snail can handle that last step for you.

First, it isn't just you

Endless feeds, autoplay and short videos that start the next one before you decide are designed to make stopping hard. If you've tried "just using it less" and it didn't stick, that's normal. The steps below work by adding small bits of friction in the right places, so stopping takes less effort than carrying on.

8 steps, from easiest to most effective

  1. Look at your real numbers. On Android, open Settings, then Digital Wellbeing, to see today's time and how many times you opened each app. On iPhone it's Screen Time. Most people guess low. Pick the one or two apps that take the most.
  2. Turn off notifications from social apps. Keep the ones from people (messages), drop likes, suggestions and "you might have missed". Every notification is an invitation back in.
  3. Move the apps off your home screen. Put them in a folder on the last page, or open them only from search. If you open them by habit, a few extra seconds is often enough to notice.
  4. Keep the phone out of the bedroom. Charge it in another room and use a cheap alarm clock. Scrolling in bed is where an hour disappears without noticing, and it eats into sleep too.
  5. Decide what you want to keep. Most people don't want to quit: they want messages, friends' stories and the odd post. Write down what's worth it. That turns "use it less" into "use it for this".
  6. Remove the endless parts, not the whole app. Reels, Shorts, TikTok's video feed and infinite timelines are where the time goes. Neither Instagram nor YouTube lets you switch them off, which is why tools like blocking Reels without deleting Instagram or blocking YouTube Shorts exist.
  7. Give each app a daily limit and open hours. A limit you can't ignore works better than a reminder you dismiss. Android's app timers pause the whole app; Cool Snail lets you set one limit for all social apps, a limit per app, and hours outside which they close.
  8. Make the rules hard to undo on a bad day. The version of you who sets limits at 10 a.m. isn't the one who wants "five more minutes" at midnight. Some tools let you lock your settings for a while, so tired-you can't loosen them.

When to ask for more help

If social media is affecting your sleep, work, relationships or mood, and these steps aren't enough, it's worth talking to a doctor or a mental health professional. Apps can add friction; they don't replace support.

Questions

How can I spend less time on social media without deleting my accounts?

Turn off notifications, move the apps off your home screen, keep the phone out of the bedroom, and remove the endless parts like Reels and Shorts instead of the whole app. Daily limits that close the app help more than reminders you can dismiss.

Why can't I stop scrolling even when I want to?

Endless feeds and autoplaying short videos are designed so there is never a natural place to stop. Adding friction, like a limit, a pause between pages or removing the short-video feed, gives you that stopping point back.

Is there an app that helps me use social media less?
